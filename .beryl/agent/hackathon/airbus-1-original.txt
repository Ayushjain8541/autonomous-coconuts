Challenge #1
“ How could we leverage AI-driven technologies for satellite collision avoidance ”
Description
With low earth orbit (LEO) being crowded with satellites and space debris, satellite operators
receive thousands of data messages daily warning of potential close encounters. Evaluating every
encounter and determining if corrective action is required necessitates too much manual labor and
is vulnerable to human error.
Want to see
A collision risk evaluator that reads orbital conjunction telemetry and subsequently calculates the
collision probability (Pc) for satellite-debris pairs, automatically ﬂagging high-risk events that
require maneuver planning.
Risk classiﬁcation: Algorithm predicting whether an event is high risk, requires maneuvering, or safe
(backed by probability)
In accordance with NASA Spacecraft Conjunction Assessment and Collision Avoidance Best
Practices Handbook (NASA/SP-20205011318) and ESA Space Debris Oﬃce Standards
Safe: Pc<10-6 and miss distance >5.0km
Elevated risk: 10-6≤Pc<10-4 or miss distance between 1.0km and 5.0km
Critical (Maneuver Required): Pc≥10-4 (i.e. risk >= -4.0 in the dataset) or miss distance <1.0km
Dashboard UI: Visual platform showing trajectories, collision risk scores and miss distances
Warnings, in 24 hour frame how many come within the deﬁned dangerous distance, generate
insights
Provided data
Collision Avoidance Challenge dataset (ﬁle in Challenge #1\archive(1).zip)
● Can use only columns event_id, time_to_tca, miss_distance, relative_speed,
relative_position_r, relative_position_t, relative_position_n , c_object_type, c_sigma_r,
t_sigma_r, max_risk_estimate, F10, AP for simplicity purposes
● In archive(1).zip:
● Use train_data to train models, ﬁt parameters, relationships between telemetry features etc.
● Has column called risk -> represents log10(pc); map this to operational risk tiers to train and
validate models

● Use test_data to generate predictions on rows, for outputs to be scored to see how well the
